# High-resolution profiling of HIV-associated immune dysregulation in East and Southern African populations at single cell level

## Description

The widespread implementation of antiretroviral therapy (ART) has led to a significant reduction in HIV incidence and HIV-related illness and death worldwide. However, individuals living with HIV (PLWH) continue to experience higher rates of both infectious and non-infectious illnesses, less effective immune responses to vaccines, and often inadequate recovery of CD4+ T-cells . The elevated incidence and prevalence of both communicable and non-communicable illnesses in PLWH despite long-term ART suggest persistent underlying immune dysfunction and variable immune recovery after ART initiation. The underlying mechanisms are not well characterised, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa, where the HIV burden is most significant. Initial studies have shown that HIV infection leads to prolonged dysregulation of specific immune cell subsets, and that the timing of ART initiation during chronic versus acute infection stages can significantly impact clinical and immunological outcomes. A better understanding of the specific mechanisms of immune dysfunction in PLWH before and after ART initiation will aid in the development of additional immunotherapies and alleviate the socioeconomic burden of managing HIV clinically for both individuals and affected countries.
